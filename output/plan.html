The one idea

A player is not worth his point total. He is worth the gap between him and the worst player you could have started instead — his replacement level. Twelve teams start one quarterback each, so the 13th-best quarterback is free, and he is very good. That makes the bar for quarterbacks brutally high.

What this means

The best quarterback in the league is worth about +99 points over a free one. The best running back is worth +194. Even in a format that inflates quarterback scoring, you should not spend an early pick on one.

The quarterback edge

This is the part almost nobody in your league will price correctly. A pass attempt is worth 0.77p − 0.50, so accuracy is paid directly. Measured over 2023–2025, the same standard production converts into your league's points at rates that differ by up to 45% between quarterbacks.

The call

Rules for draft day

1

Rounds 1–2: best running back or receiver. Full stop.

This is where the curve is steepest and where your season is decided.

2

Kicker and defense in the last two rounds only.

The gap between the best defense and a free one is about 50 points across a whole season — less than one good starter gains you in a month.

3

Watch tiers, not ranks.

The 4th and 5th best running back are indistinguishable. The gap between the last player in a tier and the first in the next one is real. When a tier is down to its last one or two and your next pick is 20 slots away, that is the moment to reach.

4

At the turn, take the scarcer position first.

Picking back-to-back, you effectively get both players. Take the one less likely to survive the round.

5

Depth is worth more than it looks.

With five bench spots and two FLEX slots, injuries and byes hit hard. Simulating the season, valuing bench depth properly beat ignoring it by a wide margin. Week 11 alone holds the byes of 10 of the top 60 players.

6

Trey McBride is the one tight end worth an early pick.

He projects 280 points against a tight end replacement level of 158. The next tier sits near 185–205. After him, tight end is a position to fill late.

How much of this should you trust?

The projections take volume and role from the market — hundreds of analysts with camp reports and depth charts beat anything I can build from box scores, and an earlier version that projected volume from player history badly underrated second-year players whose roles were changing. What the model adds is the part the market does not price: converting each player's own statistical profile into your scoring rules.

Two honest caveats. Simulated win rates against consensus-following opponents are flattered, because the model's own projections define truth inside the simulation. And because volume comes from consensus, this board will be wrong wherever the consensus is wrong.
